About me

Hi! I’m Renée, a first-year PhD student in Psychology at Stanford University, working with Prof. Steven Roberts.

Before grad school, I attended Xavier University of Louisiana, where I double majored in neuroscience and French (and took a lot of fun theology courses along the way). I am very grateful to have had amazing professors that encouraged me to put my study of science in context with other disciplines and to appreciate the many ways of knowing.

My interest in how people think about power began in French class. Hearing my professor describe Michel Foucault’s ideas about power made me wonder how people are able to sense and recognize power differences in the relationships we encounter everyday. That curiosity grew during my time as a lab manager at Princeton, where I worked on a project about children’s expectations of leaders. Our experiments asked children what they thought about an established power structure, but I also wanted to understand how they think those structures come to be. How do children reason about social hierarchies? How do they think about factors such as merit and legitimacy in determining who gains power? These are some of the questions I hope to explore as a graduate student :)

A bit more about me

I grew up in Indiana, and my family is originally from Ghana. Outside of research, I love talking about music and attending live music events, trying out new restaurants with friends, and keeping up with soccer.

Open Science & Resources

So much of getting into research runs on unwritten rules. Here are resources that helped me, in case they help you too.

C:\grad_apps
  • Application Statement Feedback Program (ASFP)

    I used this last time I applied, and it was great! They match you with reviewers in your discipline, and it’s nice to get an extra set of eyes on your materials.

  • NextGen Psych Scholars Program
  • Científico Latino

    Their mentorship program, plus lots of resources on their website about applications.

  • Mitch Prinstein’s grad school advice

    Even though I wasn’t applying to clinical psych programs, his advice made me less scared about the process. Highly recommend!

  • Guide to applying to grad school

    A guide I highly recommend.

  • Prof. Jamil Zaki’s guide to applying

    “So you’re applying for a PhD in psychology.”

  • Tip: start an academic Twitter

    Follow psychologists you look up to. Scientists often post and repost really helpful things (programs, advice, a friend who’s accepting students or has a lab opening), and you’ll often hear about it there first.

C:\predoc_jobs
  • Duke RA job board
  • Harvard Psych RA job board
  • Tip: check academic Twitter for openings

    Also look into joining a society’s listserv to hear about open positions for RAs, lab managers, postdocs, and more.
